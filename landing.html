📋 Case Summary

Barakat's client — the defendant and appellant — faced a default final judgment entered without valid service of process and containing arithmetically unvalidated damages in a commercial real estate fraud dispute. The plaintiff obtained the default at the trial level while Barakat's client was unaware of the proceedings due to defective service.

Barakat filed an Emergency Motion to Set Aside Default Final Judgment demonstrating a complete lack of valid service and errors in the unliquidated damage calculations. The lower court denied the motion. Barakat immediately invoked appellate jurisdiction before the Third District Court of Appeal. After a comprehensive omnibus brief and live oral argument, the Third DCA issued a full written reversal — completely vacating the default judgment and restoring the client's right to defend.

Key finding: The Third DCA found the trial court violated civil procedure rules governing valid service of process and the mandatory arithmetical validation required before entry of an unliquidated default judgment. The order was vacated in its entirety.

⚖️ Core Legal Issues
1
Defective Service of Process
The defendant-appellant was never validly served. The plaintiff obtained a default judgment without constitutionally adequate service — a foundational requirement before any court can exercise jurisdiction over a defendant.
2
Unliquidated Damages — Mandatory Arithmetical Validation
Florida law requires that when a plaintiff seeks an unliquidated damages default judgment, the trial court must hold an evidentiary hearing and apply arithmetical validation. No such validation occurred here.
3
Procedural Due Process — Right to be Heard
The Fourteenth Amendment guarantees every party notice and an opportunity to be heard before judgment. The combined service and damages procedure violations constituted a fundamental due process violation compelling reversal.
